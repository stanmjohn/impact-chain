# Impact chain: Skills to Work, a sixteen-week training program with a paid internship (made up)

> **PLACEHOLDER. This program is made up.** A real file would use the program's own enrollment and completion records, and state wage records for applicants who won and lost the lottery.

Built for adults 18 to 30 who applied, were found eligible, and won a seat by lottery · One US metro area · Enrolled in one year, followed for three years

## The measurement plan

- **Measured today.** None.
- **Modeled.** Kept share.
- **Assumed.** Uptake, staying and income raised.
- **From a benchmark.** None.
- **Not in the file.** Reach. The effect is inside the gain figure, which was measured in dollars against a comparison group.

**No link in this file has been measured or counted yet.** The chain leans on guesses and a tax and benefit model, and the page marks each number.

**What the evaluation budget buys.** The number that moves the answer most is income raised ("Earnings added per person offered a seat"), and nobody has measured it. Measuring that one link does more for this page than measuring any other.

## The read

Every dollar spent puts about **$0.90** in a household's hands after taxes and lost benefits, over the three years after the lottery, the training months included, in the middle run. The middle 80 percent of 20,000 runs fall between **$0.57** and **$1.23**.

**Earned and kept are different numbers.** The households earned $8,750,000 more in all and kept $6,930,000 of it, 79.2%, once taxes rose and cash benefits fell. The page counts what they kept. One adult, age 30, in PA, earning $24,000 before the raise, a household chosen by the author of this file.

**This figure rests on 2 numbers nobody has measured for this program.** They are income raised ("Earnings added per person offered a seat") and kept share ("Share of the raise the household keeps after taxes and lost cash benefits").

**Where people leave.** The most people leave at staying ("Finished the training, as a share of those who started"). About 225 of the 900 who got that far go no further in the middle run, 25%. The gain was measured over a group that already includes these people, so the loss is inside that figure and is not subtracted again.

**Shown, not multiplied in.** Uptake and staying. The gain in this file was measured over everyone at the start, so it already counts the people who never got that far. Multiplying by those links again would subtract them twice.

**Limited by seats.** The program has one seat for every four eligible applicants (made up). When applicants outnumber seats, reach and uptake are not what holds the program back, and the chain starts at the seat.

## What the household keeps

A raise of $3,000 a year leaves $2,376 in cash after federal income tax after credits $790 to $1,090, state income tax $737 to $829, payroll tax, employee side $1,853 to $2,084. Cash kept goes from $20,620 to $22,997. Shown apart and not counted, ACA premium subsidy $5,280 to $4,903. The model applies the rules to this one household and says nothing about what it filed for.

Kept counts cash and near-cash only, federal and state income tax after credits, the employee side of payroll tax, SNAP, TANF, and SSI. Health coverage and its subsidies are shown apart and never added in. The figure comes from an open tax and benefit model applying 2026 rules to one named household, and the page rebuilds from the number written in the file, not from a live call.

## The time window

- **Earnings added per person offered a seat.** Window: over the three years after the lottery, the training months included. Measured at: state wage records three years after the lottery, planned. One-time or recurring: recurring. Earnings dip in the training months and rise after.

The page counts only the window the file states and stretches nothing past it. Later dollars are not discounted. Income raised and costs cut are kept apart, because wage and benefit records can check the first and the second is mostly self-reported.

## How the range was made

Each number above was drawn 20,000 times from a triangle-shaped spread that never goes below its low or above its high and lands most often near its likely value. That triangle is drawn between a low, a likely, and a high, and it is not a statistical confidence interval. Each run walks the chain from the start to the end. The middle run is the median and the range is the 10th to the 90th percentile. The seed is 20260918, so the same file gives the same page on any machine.

## What this page refuses to do

It never prints a final figure without a range. It never counts an effect with no comparison group, or one that does not say who it was measured over. It never puts dollars on a result nobody measured in dollars. It never stretches a gain past the window someone measured. It never names a step for improvement without a published benchmark to hold it against. It never compares this program to another one. It never uses a benchmark without naming it and its source.
