# Impact chain: Pantry Text, a text-message SNAP screener (made up)

> **PLACEHOLDER. This program is made up.** A real file would use the pantry network's own text logs for reach, screener logs for uptake and staying, and a group texted three months later for the effect.

Built for households who use food pantries in one county, look eligible for SNAP, and are not enrolled · One US county · The first grant year

## The measurement plan

- **Measured today.** None.
- **Assumed.** Reach, uptake, staying and effect.
- **From a benchmark.** Income raised.
- **Not in the file.** None.

**No link in this file has been measured or counted yet.** The chain leans on guesses and benchmarks, and the page marks each number.

**What the evaluation budget buys.** The number that moves the answer most is uptake ("Started the screener"), and nobody has measured it. Measuring that one link does more for this page than measuring any other.

## The read

Every dollar spent puts about **$6.99** in a household's hands, over the first twelve months on SNAP, in the middle run. The middle 80 percent of 20,000 runs fall between **$4.41** and **$10.99**.

Of 1,000 people the program was built for, about **32** end up with SNAP benefits they would not have had anyway, 22 to 46 across the middle runs.

**This figure rests on 5 numbers nobody has measured for this program.** They are reach ("Opened the text message"), uptake ("Started the screener"), staying ("Finished the screener and sent in an application"), effect ("Approved for SNAP who would not have enrolled anyway, among those who sent in an application") and income raised ("SNAP benefits per newly enrolled household").

## The time window

- **SNAP benefits per newly enrolled household.** Window: over the first twelve months on SNAP. Measured at: benefit amounts in the benchmark study, state records. One-time or recurring: recurring monthly, until the household has to recertify.

The page counts only the window the file states and stretches nothing past it. Later dollars are not discounted. Income raised and costs cut are kept apart, because wage and benefit records can check the first and the second is mostly self-reported.

**Share of people sent outreach who respond**, used to check "Opened the text message". Low and high are the lowest and highest of the four points below. Likely is the midpoint of the two middle points.

What it does not cover. Outreach of any kind other than a letter, a text, an email, or a public launch. The four points reach very different groups. Older adults in Pennsylvania by mail, people in Los Angeles who had already applied for SNAP, and taxpayers in twelve states who could use a new federal filing tool. The Los Angeles group had already applied, so it responds far more often than a cold list. Some people who started the federal filing checker lived outside the pilot states, so that point runs high against its own base.

## How the range was made

Each number above was drawn 20,000 times from a triangle-shaped spread that never goes below its low or above its high and lands most often near its likely value. That triangle is drawn between a low, a likely, and a high, and it is not a statistical confidence interval. Each run walks the chain from the start to the end. The middle run is the median and the range is the 10th to the 90th percentile. The seed is 20260918, so the same file gives the same page on any machine.

## What this page refuses to do

It never prints a final figure without a range. It never counts an effect with no comparison group, or one that does not say who it was measured over. It never puts dollars on a result nobody measured in dollars. It never stretches a gain past the window someone measured. It never names a step for improvement without a published benchmark to hold it against. It never compares this program to another one. It never uses a benchmark without naming it and its source.
